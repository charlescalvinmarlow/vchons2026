Project Synopsis

Digital Disembodiment is a four-part project — Mechanical Flipbook, digital video, publication and poster — that considers how cinema has depicted the ever-evolving relationship between humans and the technologies we create. As emerging technologies and digital software allow individuals to form avatars of themselves in cyberspace, our material relationship to identity, the body, and the environment continues to transform. Remediation — the process of transferring content or meaning from one medium to another — underpins Digital Disembodiment. The central part of the project, the Mechanical Flipbook, reflects this process. A mechanism reconstructed based on pre-existing motion picture devices; viewers can crank the handle of the Flipbook, translating the image into a new medium. This act encourages a direct relationship between individual and machine, creating a parallel between the films' subjects and the viewer.

A digital video, publication and poster accompany the Flipbook. The digital video provides a reference that allows viewers to assess the affordances and limitations of translating digital to analogue. The poster and publication provide a grounding framework for both the digital video and the Mechanical Flipbook. The publication contains a research paper and analyses of twelve films that explore how the ‘self’ has been modified through technology, and how our relationship to the body, the environment and each are constantly evolving. Concerns surrounding Artificial Intelligence, surveillance, posthumanism and the future of design practice are also embedded in the analysis of the films. Digital Disembodiment is a meditative projection. Rather than pursuing a singular answer or solution, it explores how we may adapt, reject, and evolve with our technologies.
