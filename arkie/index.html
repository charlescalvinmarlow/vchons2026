Project Synopsis

Life is only becoming weirder and weirder. Why not embrace it?

While researching critical theorist Sianne Ngai’s aesthetic categories of Cute, Interesting and Zany (2012), I came across the weirdness-openness paradigm (2025), which argues that countercultures can foster individual agency by pushing boundaries of what we consider beautiful.

This inspired me to conduct a series of research through design experiments (see the documentation publication) exploring how embracing weirdness and eccentricity can combat contemporary aesthetic trends like the fetishisation of numbness: trends like ‘lobotomy chic’ and the ‘dissassociative pout’ being glorified in current media.

Embracing weirdness is empowering; a means of unapologetic self-expression, allowing women to exist for themselves, rather than for others. To explore the power of weirdness in combatting numbness, and in the hope of inspiring other girls to be weirder, I researched and designed a case study on a fellow weird girl, Shrimp Barbie (Holly Mckee). I coded a website to present a series of video interviews and posters I designed that feel authentic to her character, and share her weirdness with the world.
