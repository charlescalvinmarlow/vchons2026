Project Synopsis

Women in need of medical care are too often dismissed or overlooked in the healthcare system; the evidence is clear, but not easy to find. Diagnosing Bias translates healthcare statistics about three common but frequently misdiagnosed or misunderstood medical conditions into personas that draw out the lived experiences of women who suffer medical injustice: Clara unknowingly lives with endometriosis for years, Rebecca has a sudden heart attack, and Florence struggles with perimenopausal symptoms. These personas, presented as scroll narratives, are based on research into these conditions, including documentation of experiences of real women.

While this project is a research-through-design experiment for my thesis that a Data Humanist approach can disrupt medical misogyny, designing Diagnosing Bias also allowed me to explore my values as an emerging designer. Creating a sensitively crafted, well-researched web project taught me the responsibility that comes with visual communication practice, and I’m eager to keep learning new ways to share important messages, ethically.
