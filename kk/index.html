KK Patel

Out of Line

KK Patel is a Sydney/Eora-based designer exploring how her Indian heritage informs her practice. Through print, publication and analogue processes, she combines colour, pattern and imagery to explore memory, nostalgia and the duality of growing up between two cultures.

Project Synopsis

Out of Line is a two-volume anthology that asks: What does it mean to decolonise my own design practice? I examine ‘universal’ Eurocentric design principles and attempt to subvert how we use them by incorporating traditional Indian design principles in my design practice.

‘VOL 1: Expanding design practice beyond Eurocentrism’ unfolds across three core studio experiments. First, I attempt to decolonise the grid through layouts developed from Indian sacred geometry, creating non-conventional typesetting for high-context cultures. Second, to celebrate Indian colour logic I created a resist printmaking technique to translate Indian fabric dyeing methods into print, to create vibrant and unexpected maximalist images. Third, a typographic experiment deconstructs Gujarati letterforms as a method for studying my mother tongue. The three experiments are accompanied by reflections that reveal biases embedded in contemporary design practice. The writing documents the challenges, vulnerability, and thinking required to shift ingrained design habits. Out of Line’s does not propose a single solution for decolonising design, nor do I advocate for discarding Western design principles entirely. Instead, I advocate for making space for different knowledges, ways of working, seeing and thinking.

‘VOL. 2: What is lost when a culture is commercialised?’ draws on theorists including Edward Said, Raymond Williams, Terry Eagleton, and Eduardo Navas to explore cultural appropriation and how cultural motifs are adapted, renamed, and reframed in Western and South Asian markets, in order to understand the line between cultural evolution and cultural erasure. This investigation into differences between ethical reinterpretation and commodification ensured I didn't accidentally appropriate my own culture.
